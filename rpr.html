What Is an RPR?

A clear picture of your
property and improvements

A Real Property Report is a legal document prepared by an Alberta Land Surveyor. It illustrates the boundaries of a property and the location of visible improvements in relation to those boundaries.

It helps property owners, buyers, sellers, lawyers, lenders, and municipalities understand how buildings and other visible improvements are situated on the parcel.

An RPR is not a building inspection, title insurance policy, permit-history report, or automatic confirmation of municipal compliance. A municipality reviews the completed RPR separately when a Certificate of Compliance is requested.

01

Property Boundaries

The surveyed parcel limits, legal description, dimensions, and relevant survey information.

02

Visible Improvements

Homes, garages, decks, sheds, fences, retaining walls, and other visible improvements where applicable.

03

Setback Distances

Measured distances from buildings and improvements to property boundaries.

04

Registered Interests

Relevant easements, rights-of-way, and other registered interests that affect how the property is shown.

05

Surveyor Certification

A signed professional opinion and plan prepared under the authority of an Alberta Land Surveyor.

When You May Need One

An RPR supports important
property decisions

Requirements vary by transaction and municipality, but these are some of the most common reasons clients contact us.

01

Selling a Property

Your lawyer, buyer, or purchase contract may require a current RPR, often with evidence of municipal compliance.

02

Buying a Property

An RPR can help show how visible buildings and improvements relate to the legal property boundaries.

03

Municipal Compliance

A municipality uses the RPR to review the location of structures against applicable land-use requirements.

04

New Improvements

Decks, garages, additions, sheds, fences, and other changes may make an older RPR no longer current.

05

Property Questions

An RPR may help identify potential encroachments or location concerns that need professional review.

06

Legal or Lending Requests

Lawyers, lenders, insurers, or other professionals may request an RPR as part of their due diligence.

RPR Updates for Existing Clients

Your previous report may
save time and money

If Vista Geomatics previously prepared an RPR for the property, contact us before ordering a completely new report. Depending on the age of the original work, available survey records, and changes to the property, an update may be possible.

Our team will review the existing file and determine what field work, drafting, title information, and professional review are required to make the report current.

An update is not automatic. The Alberta Land Surveyor must confirm that the prior information is suitable and that the revised report accurately reflects the property today.

Tell Us It Is an Update

Mention that Vista completed an earlier RPR and provide the property address.

Share What Changed

Let us know about additions, decks, garages, sheds, fences, retaining walls, or other improvements.

We Review the File

Our team checks the prior report and survey records before confirming the appropriate scope.

Only the Work Needed

When an update is suitable, reusing reliable information may reduce duplicated work.
